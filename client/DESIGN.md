# Design System: Atlas

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Quiet Register"**

Atlas is the category standard for chat-with-your-documents tools, built to the craft of Claude.ai's calm reading column, Perplexity's numbered sources and Linear's dense, quiet chrome with the content on an inset sheet. The owner chose the conventions on purpose and the build doesn't dress them up. The palette is grounded in paper, pencil and a desk's manila folders: graphite and warm stone, with no blue cast. The 52px top bar and the sidebar (300px by default, dragged between 240 and 480px, or closed) are one continuous stone chrome field. The conversation sits on it as an inset sheet: the page ground, a 1px hairline and 8px corners, scrolling on its own while the chrome stays still. The sheet holds one 760px column. Everything is drawn with 1px hairlines on flat grounds. The one graphite appears only where the reader acts or where the system points at evidence.

The same world runs in two schemes and two languages. Dark is the same roles on warm graphite: the chrome darkest, the page a step up, raised surfaces a step above that. Every gray, ink, amber and red shade is a role, not a lightness step, and each role has its dark value, so a component that asks for "dimmed" or "red tint" reads correctly in both. The primary inverts by scheme: a graphite fill with white text in light, a chalk fill with graphite text in dark. Arabic mirrors the whole layout, while content (answers, questions, quotes, table cells, file names) keeps its own direction. Figures are Western digits in both languages, so a number on screen matches the number in the file.

Density follows Linear more than a marketing page. Tree rows are 13px names over 12px meta. Tables run at 13px with tabular figures. The answer reads at 15px with a 75ch measure. Motion only marks state changes: a hover tint, a chevron turning, a folder opening, the sidebar sliding, and the finished answer's table and sources settling in once with a short fade-and-rise. Two exceptions, both approved by the owner on 2026-10-29, and both are Atlas's face (a philosopher's bust in graphite line work, marble in both schemes): the opening of every fresh conversation, where the bust looks up, says its line and waits for the question, and a voice call, during which the face moves continuously.

The system refuses the demo look named in the direction contract: decorative gradients, glowing AI sparkles, bubble chat and cards inside cards. The question is a heading-weight line, not a bubble. The answer is plain prose on the page ground, not a card.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One brand tone (graphite, a near-black at OKLCH hue 85 with almost no chroma, shade 7 of a 10-step ramp in both schemes; chalk in dark) for actions, focus, selection, links, citations, the open file, the streaming caret and the call avatar's ring while Atlas speaks
- Warm stone neutrals in both schemes; nothing reads blue
- One chrome field (top bar and sidebar) with the conversation on an inset sheet of page ground; the sidebar closes and resizes
- Light and dark schemes from one set of colour roles; the PDF page and the bust stay white in both
- File kinds wear the colour they are known by on their icons only: PDF red, Excel green, manila folders
- 1px hairline borders instead of shadows on every resting surface; floating surfaces also take a hairline edge in dark
- One system UI family for everything (Segoe UI carries Arabic on Windows); monospace only for SQL and tool input
- Tabular Western figures wherever numbers appear, in English and Arabic
- The whole layout mirrors in Arabic; content keeps its own direction
- Continuous motion lives only in the bust: in the empty conversation, and in the call avatar while a call is open

## Colors

A warm, nearly colourless palette: stone grays carry the structure, one graphite carries meaning, amber and red appear only when a file or request has a problem, and each file kind wears its own colour on its icon. Each role below has a light value and a dark value (the `-dark` tokens); components name the role and the scheme supplies the value.

### Primary
- **Graphite** (`ink`, the Mantine palette still named `ink`, shade 7): filled primary buttons (Load, Send, Stop), the composer's phone, the dictation check mark and End call, the active source number, a cited sheet row's number, the 3px streaming caret, and the sidebar edge while it is dragged or focused. White text on it reads at 12.2:1. It hovers to shade 8 (`ink-hover`). In light, focus outlines, links, the input caret and the call avatar's speaking ring use the same graphite.
- **Chalk** (`ink-dark`, `ink-hover-dark`, dark only): primaryShade is 7 in both schemes, and dark remaps ink 7 and 8 to chalk, so the filled action reads as a light key on the dark page, with graphite text at 14.4:1.
- **On Graphite** (`on-ink`, `on-ink-dark`; `--atlas-on-ink`): the text on any primary fill, white in light and the dark page tone (dark 7) in dark. Mantine gives a filled control fixed white text, so the theme sets this token on filled Button and ActionIcon in the primary through `vars`. The active source number and the cited sheet row number take it too.
- **Focus and links in dark** (`focus-dark`, `body-text-dark`): in dark, focus outlines, the input caret and the model picker's check use `#c6c4c0`, and links and the call avatar's speaking ring take the anchor colour, dark 0. Graphite links sit close to body text, so every link carries its underline in both schemes (Anchor `underline: 'always'`; plain links 1px at a 0.2em offset).
- **Graphite Tint** (`ink-tint`, `ink-tint-dark`, ink 1): the open source row, cited rows in the sheet viewer, `<mark>` highlights, and the ground of Mantine's light variant.
- **Graphite Selection** (`ink-selection`, `ink-selection-dark`, ink 2): the text selection ground, with Graphite Deep (`ink-deep`, `ink-deep-dark`, ink 9) text, and the file open in the viewer in the folder tree, deep enough to read on the chrome, with its name in the bright tone. The light variant hovers to it.
- **Graphite Wash** (`ink-wash`, `ink-wash-dark`, ink 0): the ramp's lightest step. No component marks with it now. In dark, ink 0, 1 and 2 sit above the hover tone (gray 0), so a marked row never reads quieter than a hovered one.
- **Pencil Mark** (`ink-mark`, ink 4): mixed at 45% over the rendered PDF page, so a cited passage looks shaded in pencil and its printed words show through. It is not remapped: the page stays white paper in both schemes, so the mark looks the same in both.

### File kinds (icons only)
- **PDF Red** (`kind-pdf`, `kind-pdf-dark`) and **Excel Green** (`kind-excel`, `kind-excel-dark`): the outline file-kind icon in the folder tree, the source rows, the skipped-files list and the viewer header. Files Atlas does not read keep a dimmed icon. Each reads at 4.6:1 or better on the chrome in both schemes.
- **Manila** (`folder-back`, `folder-front`, `folder-edge`, each with its `-dark` value): the two-tone folder glyph in the tree, a deeper back panel with its tab, a lighter front and a thin edge that holds the shape on the light chrome.

### Tertiary (status only)
- **Amber** (`amber-tint` ground, `amber-text` text, `amber-wash`/`amber-rule`/`amber-icon` in notices, each with its `-dark` value): files Atlas read only partly (Scanned, Too large) and warning notices. The light badge text reads at 7.7:1.
- **Signal Red** (`red-tint` ground, `red-text` text, `red-wash`/`red-rule`/`red-icon` in notices, each with its `-dark` value): files Atlas could not open (Damaged, Password protected) and error notices. `red-error` is the inline error text under fields and the dropzone. In dark the grounds sink to deep brown and maroon and the text and icons lift to light amber and coral.

### Neutral
- **Chrome Ground** (`chrome-ground`, `chrome-ground-dark`): the one field behind the top bar, the sidebar and the sheet's margin, plus the viewer stage behind a PDF page, sheet headers, the row-number gutter, the strip of sheet tabs and the code ground. In dark it is the deepest graphite.
- **Page Ground** (`page-ground`, `page-ground-dark`): the inset sheet the conversation sits on. The docked composer strip reuses it so turns scroll under it cleanly.
- **Surface** (`white`, `surface-dark`): raised surfaces that hold content you read: composer box, call card, result-table frame, sheet stage, the open sheet's tab, modals, menus, popovers and dropdowns. On the chrome ground, a tree row lifts to it under the pointer. In dark it is a step above the page, so these surfaces lift off it.
- **Hover Ground** (`hover-ground`, `hover-ground-dark`): the hover tint on rows and quiet controls, sticky table headers, the disabled composer, the call avatar's disc and the "not found" notice ground.
- **Quiet Chip** (`quiet-chip`, `quiet-chip-dark`): neutral badges, source numbers and the disabled-control ground. Also the hairline under the viewer header.
- **Hairline** (`hairline`, `hairline-dark`): the sheet's edge, table frames, quote rules, tree rails, the trace rail, the PDF page edge, the sheet gutter rule, the top bar's divider before the language and theme, and the disabled-control border.
- **Border** (`border`, `border-dark`): input and composer borders, the default control border, scrollbar thumbs (browser and ScrollArea) and trace markers. In dark it is also the hairline edge of floating surfaces.
- **Dimmed** (`dimmed`, `dimmed-dark`): meta text, placeholders (`placeholder-dark` in dark), column headers, the icon of a file Atlas does not read, chevrons and list markers (5.1:1 on chrome in light).
- **Body Text** (`body-text`, `body-text-dark`): body text. Headings, the wordmark, the folder root, the open file's name, the opening line and questions take the bright tone (`body-text` in light, `bright-dark` in dark). `soft-text` and `secondary-text` step down for row text, folder rows and quiet controls. `faint-glyph` is for trailing arrows, empty-cell dashes, disabled text and the sidebar edge under the pointer only.
- **Overlay** (`overlay`, `overlay-dark`): the veil behind modals, 32% graphite in light and a deeper 62% near-black in dark.
- **Tooltip**: the inverse of the scheme: Body Text ground with white text in light, `body-text-dark` ground with `page-ground-dark` text in dark.

### Named Rules
**The Graphite Is Evidence Rule.** Graphite marks only what you can act on and where an answer points (primary action, focus, link, selection, active citation, cited text and cited rows, the open file, streaming caret, the sidebar edge while it moves, and the call avatar's ring while Atlas speaks). The wordmark and its mark, headings, icons and the drawing of the bust stay neutral.

**The Colour Means Trouble Rule.** Amber and red mean a file or request has a problem, nothing else. The one other use of colour is a file's kind, on its icon only: PDF red, Excel green, folders manila. A kind colour never reaches a name, a badge, a row ground or a notice. Document types and the ok state use neutral badges, empty and skipped files use gray, and "not found" answers use a neutral notice.

**The Roles Not Shades Rule.** A component asks for a role (gray 6 for dimmed, red 1 for the error tint, `--atlas-surface` for a raised surface, `--atlas-on-ink` for text on the primary, `--atlas-kind-pdf` for a PDF icon) and never for a lightness. The dark scheme gives every role its own value, so nothing is coloured per scheme in a component. The only fixed values are the ones that are real objects: the white PDF page and the marble bust.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Segoe UI (with system-ui, -apple-system, Helvetica Neue, Noto Sans, Noto Sans Arabic, Geeza Pro, Tahoma, Arial)
**Body Font:** the same stack
**Label/Mono Font:** ui-monospace (with Cascadia Mono, Consolas, SF Mono, Menlo), for SQL and tool input in the trace only

**Character:** One native family at about a 1.15 ratio (12, 13, 15, 17, 20, 26). It looks like the user's own operating system in either script, so the documents and figures carry the voice. Weight does the ranking: 400 for reading, 600 for names, labels and headings, and 700 only on the wordmark.

### Hierarchy
- **Opening quote** (400, 26px, 1.3, -0.01em, balanced wrap, at most 20ch): Atlas's line in the empty conversation, in the bright tone, and the largest type in the app. 20px on a phone. Arabic sets it at 1.55 with no tracking.
- **Headline** (600, 20px, 1.35): each question at the top of its turn, `dir="auto"` so Arabic questions set right to left.
- **Title** (600, 17px, 1.5): modal titles and headings inside an answer. The wordmark uses the same size at 700 with -0.015em tracking.
- **Body** (400, 15px, 1.55, max 75ch): answer prose and composer text. Arabic answers set at 1.07x (about 16px) with 1.75 line height. Arabic interface text takes a 1.6 body line height, because Arabic letters sit lower and denser than Latin at the same size.
- **Body small** (400, 13px, 1.45): tree names, source rows, tables, trace steps, form labels (600), the opening's "— Atlas" (600) and the usage line.
- **Label** (600, 12px, sentence case, no tracking): badges, source numbers, the "Sources" list heading, sheet column letters and row numbers. Dimmed 12px/400 is the meta line.

### Named Rules
**The Tabular Figures Rule.** Every place a number appears sets `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Numeric table columns align right and never wrap.

**The Western Digits Rule.** Numbers are Western digits with comma thousands separators in both languages (counts, sizes, pages, amounts, times), the same as the documents and answers. Amounts are shown exactly as the data holds them, never rounded. Arabic counts use the noun's own plural form around the figure ("ملفان", "3 ملفات", "11 ملفًا").

**The Sentence Case Rule.** Labels and badges are sentence case at zero tracking. The build has no uppercase or letterspaced text.

## Layout

Fixed app frame: a 52px top bar and the sidebar (8px top, 16px sides and foot) on one chrome field, with no rule between them. The main area holds the inset sheet: full height under the top bar less an 8px foot, 8px clear of the window's end edge, with a 1px hairline and 8px corners. The sheet scrolls on its own and contains its overscroll, so the top bar and sidebar never move. Below Mantine's `sm` breakpoint (48em) the sidebar folds behind a burger and the sheet goes edge to edge under the top bar, keeping only its top hairline and losing its corners.

The sidebar is 300px by default. On a wide screen it closes from the toggle at the start of the top bar, or with Ctrl+B (Cmd+B on a Mac), and slides on AppShell's 180ms. Its edge drags to resize it with Mantine's `useAppShellResize`, between 240 and 480px. A drag narrower than 160px closes it, and it reopens at the width it had before; double-clicking the edge returns it to 300px. Its width and open state are kept in localStorage (`atlas-sidebar`), and a missing or out-of-range value falls back to open at 300px. While the sidebar is hidden it is `inert`, so its controls never take focus. With it closed, the sheet takes an 8px start margin as well, so it stands off both window edges; the margin rides the same AppShell timing, which drops to zero while the edge is dragged, and does not animate under reduced motion. A phone always starts with the sidebar folded away.

Inside the sheet, a centred column of 760px plus 16px gutters each side. Its content stack has 32px top and 24px bottom padding with 24px between turns. Inside a turn, parts are 16px apart.

Before the first question the sheet holds only Atlas's opening: the bust, its line and the byline, centred in the space above the composer and lifted 8dvh off the middle. The composer always docks at the bottom of the sheet with `position: sticky` on the page ground, 8px above it and 16px below; on a fresh page it waits below the sheet's foot until the line is said, then rises into place. The column clips what hangs below it (`overflow-y: clip`, which makes no scroll container, so the dock still sticks), so the rising box never adds a scrollbar. Turns that scroll under it fade out over a 24px band of page ground just above the dock (see The Fade Above The Dock Rule).

Spacing runs on an 8/12/16/24/32 scale (`xs` to `xl`). Tight inner rhythm uses 2, 4 and 6px steps: 2px between a name and its meta, 4px between badges and trace lines, and 6px for row padding and stacked label-to-field gaps. Source rows bleed their hover ground 8px past the column edge so their text lines up with the prose. Result tables scroll inside their frame up to `min(28rem, 60dvh)`.

Below `sm`, the sidebar toggle gives way to the burger, the language and theme controls move from the top bar to the sidebar's foot under a hairline, Clear chat becomes an icon button, the cost-and-time pair moves to the composer's hint line, and the viewer goes full screen. Ctrl+B opens and closes the folded sidebar there too. Below `xs` (36em), a source's place always drops under its name.

During a voice call the call card takes the composer's place in the dock, at the same width, and the opening steps back while it is open. Inside it, 12px padding and 12px gaps: the avatar and a text block side by side, then a control row under a 1px Quiet Chip rule (the call's voice at the start, actions at the end). Below `xs` the avatar drops to 56px, controls grow to 40px, the voice's name takes the row's remaining width so the controls stay on one line, only the latest caption shows, and the standing hint under the card is hidden.

Content widths inside the viewer cap at 560 to 600px for notes and states. The PDF page centres on the chrome stage with 16px padding, and the sheet runs edge to edge on the surface like a grid in Excel. A workbook opened whole keeps its sheet tabs along the viewer's foot, as Excel does.

### Arabic and direction
The interface language sets `dir` on the document, and the whole layout mirrors: the sidebar moves to the right, its resize edge and the toggle's panel icon follow it, the top bar's ends swap, notifications rise from the bottom corner at the end of the reading direction, and every spacing, rule and rail uses logical properties (`inline-start`, `inline-end`). Arrows and chevrons that point along the reading direction turn round (`flip-rtl`, or a rotation for the trace and tree chevrons).

Content keeps its own direction with `dir="auto"`: answers (set right to left when Arabic, with `lang`), questions, quotes, table cells, sheet cells, file names and said-aloud transcripts. A result table runs in the direction of its own column labels, not the page's. SQL, formulas, tool input that is code, folder paths and the full-path tooltip are always left to right. The Excel grid in the viewer stays left to right as Excel draws it, while its notes, buttons and sheet tabs follow the interface. Numbers in tables align right physically, not to the end, so their units line up in either direction. A Latin file name in an Arabic tree keeps its own direction but lines up on the right with the Arabic lines.

Inside an Arabic sentence, file names, sheet names, paths, extensions and codes are wrapped in first-strong isolates, so their dots, slashes and colons stay put. An amount and its currency, and a time and its unit, are joined by no-break spaces so a wrapping line never strands "SAR" or "s" alone. The wordmark stays "Atlas" in Latin, and so does the name under the opening line; the keyboard shortcut in the sidebar toggle's tooltip stays left to right.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat, with tonal layering. Resting surfaces separate by ground (chrome, page, surface) and 1px hairlines, never by shadow; the inset sheet itself is a hairline and a change of ground, with no shadow. Shadows are reserved for things that float above the page: modals, menus, popovers, comboboxes and the PDF page sitting on its stage. They carry a graphite tint (`rgba(27, 25, 23, a)`), a downward offset and a soft negative-spread blur.

A shadow does not read on a near-black page, so in dark every floating surface also takes a 1px edge in the Border role (`--atlas-float-edge`, transparent in light), sits on the raised surface rather than the page, and its veil is the deeper dark overlay.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Page** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(27,25,23,0.06)`): the rendered PDF page on the viewer stage.
- **Overlay** (`box-shadow: 0 6px 16px -4px rgba(27,25,23,0.14), 0 2px 4px rgba(27,25,23,0.05)`): menus, popovers, comboboxes (model picker, voice picker).
- **Modal** (`box-shadow: 0 12px 32px -8px rgba(27,25,23,0.2), 0 4px 8px rgba(27,25,23,0.06)`): the viewer and confirmation modals, over the Overlay veil.

### Named Rules
**The Hairline Not Shadow Rule.** A surface that sits in the page gets a 1px border and no shadow. A shadow means the surface floats above the page, and in dark a floating surface also carries a hairline edge.

**The Fade Above The Dock Rule.** The one gradient in the system is functional: a 24px band above the docked composer that runs from transparent to the page ground, so turns scrolling under the composer fade out instead of being cut at its edge. It uses only the page ground, never a hue, and it is never decoration. No other surface takes a gradient.

## Shapes

Small, even corners on a 4/6/8 scale, with 6px as the default. Chips, source numbers, `<mark>`, tree rows and hover rows take 4px. Buttons, inputs, menus, notices and the result-table frame take 6px. The inset sheet, the composer, the call card and modals take 8px. The PDF page takes 4px, so it reads as paper. Only the trace markers (18px circles) and the bust's medallion are round. Borders are always 1px. The trace rail and the tree's rails are 1px lines; the trace rail turns dashed while a step runs, and the avatar's ring follows the same rule: dashed (4 on, 5 off) while a question runs. Refused or unfinished markers have dashed rings. Quotes are set off by a 1px rule on their reading side (`border-inline-start`), so they mirror in Arabic. The quote rule is a quiet hairline in `hairline` gray, not a coloured stripe.

The mark is a spreadsheet and a PDF fanned behind Atlas's face (20px): the files in the same neutral bright tone as the wordmark beside it, 8px apart, the spreadsheet leaning left with its grid cut out and the PDF leaning right in front with its lines cut out and its folded corner at 42% for the fold's shade; the call face's head sits over them in its marble, the same in both schemes, held clear by a gap. The head is drawn from the call face's geometry in the small call face's style (no curls, heavier lines, larger eyes) so it reads at 20px (`shell/AtlasMark.tsx`).

The folder glyph is drawn for Atlas rather than taken from Tabler: an 18px manila folder on a 20-unit grid, a back panel with its tab and a front panel with 1.2 to 1.5-unit corners, held by a 0.9-unit edge with round joins. Closed, the front covers the back below the tab; open, the front drops and leans forward so the back shows above it.

### Opening

- **Beats** (about 1.6s for the five-word line, all on `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`): the face comes into focus over 760ms (from 92% scale, 10px blur and no opacity) with its eyes lowered; at 380ms it looks up; at 760ms it speaks, each word rising 6px out of a 4px blur over 460ms, 150ms after the one before, while its mouth opens in 112ms syllables. It returns over 320ms when the box is empty again, without saying the line again. Clearing the chat plays the opening again; the composer stays docked.

- **Reduced motion:** the still face, the whole line and the composer are there from the first frame, with no focus pull, rising words or rising box; stepping back is a plain fade.
- **Access:** a figure with the line as a blockquote and the byline as its caption, read like any content; hidden from assistive technology while it steps back.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take every colour, size, radius and shadow from the Mantine theme (`theme.ts`). Components carry no hex values.
- **Do** ask for colour roles (gray 0 to 9, ink, yellow and red shades, `--atlas-surface`, `--atlas-focus`, `--atlas-on-ink`, `--atlas-kind-*`, `--atlas-folder-*`) so each surface reads in both schemes (The Roles Not Shades Rule).
- **Do** keep graphite to actions, focus, selection, links, citations, cited rows, the open file, the streaming caret, the sidebar edge while it moves and the call avatar's speaking ring (The Graphite Is Evidence Rule).
- **Do** put text on a primary fill in `--atlas-on-ink`, so it is white on graphite and graphite on chalk.
- **Do** underline every link; graphite alone does not set a link apart from body text.
- **Do** separate resting surfaces with 1px hairlines on chrome, page or surface grounds, and keep shadows for modals, menus and the PDF page; give floating surfaces a hairline edge in dark.
- **Do** keep the conversation on the inset sheet, which scrolls on its own inside the still chrome field, and keep it off both window edges when the sidebar is closed.
- **Do** make a hidden sidebar `inert`, and keep its width and open state for the next visit.
- **Do** dock the question box at the sheet's foot from the start, with the page-ground fade above it, and let the opening hold the sheet until a question is written.
- **Do** set every figure in tabular Western digits and right-align numeric columns physically, in both languages.
- **Do** use logical properties so the whole layout mirrors in Arabic, `dir="auto"` on content, and isolates around file names, paths and codes inside Arabic sentences.
- **Do** keep SQL, formulas, paths and the Excel grid left to right in both languages.
- **Do** join an amount and its currency, and a number and its unit, with a no-break space.
- **Do** use 150ms ease-out for hover and colour changes and 180ms `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for content settling in, collapsing, a folder opening or the sidebar sliding, and disable both under reduced motion.
- **Do** use Tabler outline icons at 1.5 to 2 stroke, sized 12 to 18px. The manila folder glyph is the one authored icon.
- **Do** give the bust a still pose per state under reduced motion, and stop its motion in a hidden tab (The Living Face Rule).
- **Do** let any click or key finish the opening's line at once, and wait for the tab to be seen before it plays.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use decorative gradients, glowing AI sparkles, chat bubbles or cards inside cards. The page-ground fade above the docked composer is the only gradient (The Fade Above The Dock Rule).
- **Don't** colour document types or ok files. Amber and red are for files and requests with problems only. A file's kind colour goes on its icon only, never on its name, badge, row or notice.
- **Don't** put a shadow on a surface that sits in the page, including the inset sheet.
- **Don't** pick a colour per scheme inside a component, or turn the PDF page or the bust dark.
- **Don't** set uppercase or letterspaced labels. Labels are sentence case at 12px/600.
- **Don't** add a second typeface or a display face. Monospace is for SQL and tool input only.
- **Don't** use Eastern Arabic digits or round an amount for display.
- **Don't** translate the wordmark, file names, item codes or numbers.
- **Don't** choreograph page loads. The one exception, approved by the owner on 2026-10-29, is the opening of a fresh conversation, where the bust says its line and the composer rises; it plays on each load and each cleared chat, never blocks the app, and any click or key finishes it. Elsewhere motion marks state changes only, except inside the call avatar while a call is open.
- **Don't** let a blue cast back into the neutrals or the brand tone. Grays and the dark ramp are warm stone; graphite has almost no chroma.
- **Don't** colour the bust. Its drawing stays graphite on white marble in both schemes, and the brand tone touches only its ring while Atlas speaks.
